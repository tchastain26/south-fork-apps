About this tool

Ukulele Fretboard

Ukulele Fretboard shows every note across four strings and twelve frets, with scale highlighting and support for standard, D, and low-G tunings. It is the fastest way to see why the ukulele fingers differently from a guitar.

How to use it

  1. Pick a root note or leave it on None for all note names.
  2. Choose a scale to highlight, from major and minor through pentatonics, blues and the modes.
  3. Switch tuning between standard GCEA, D tuning, and low G.
  4. Read the highlighted notes across the neck.

Reentrant tuning, and why it matters

Standard ukulele tuning is GCEA, but the G is normally reentrant, meaning it is tuned above the C rather than below it. The strings do not run low to high in pitch order, which is what gives the instrument its bright, chiming sound.

Low G tuning drops that string an octave so the instrument runs in pitch order like a guitar. The fingerings are identical, but scale runs and bass notes sound completely different.

Worked example

Mapping C major, the friendliest key on a ukulele:

  • Root NoteC
  • ScaleMajor
  • TuningStandard (GCEA)

Result: The scale highlights on the open C string and across the first three frets, which is why so many beginner ukulele songs sit in C.

When it helps

  • Understanding why a guitar shape does not transfer directly to a ukulele.
  • Deciding whether low G is worth restringing for.
  • Finding scale notes for a melody rather than only strumming chords.
  • Learning the neck past the third fret.

Common mistakes

  • Treating the top four guitar strings as identical. Ukulele is tuned a fourth higher, so the same shape gives a different chord name.
  • Expecting the open G to be the lowest note in standard tuning. In reentrant tuning it is not.
